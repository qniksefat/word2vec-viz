01

The idea: meaning from company

“You shall know a word by the company it keeps.”

— J.R. Firth, 1957

CBOW turns that sentence into a training task. Hide one word, show the network the 2 words on each side, and ask it to guess the missing middle word. Do this a million times.

The network can only get good at this game if words that show up in similar contexts end up with similar vectors. So good, great and excellent drift together, as do movie, film and flick. The guessing task is just an excuse; the vectors are the real product.

Try it yourself → these are real windows from the corpus that our trained model gets right.

02

Text → tokens → ids

Each review is lowercased, HTML line breaks are stripped, the text is split on whitespace, and only purely alphabetic pieces survive (so movie. or don't are dropped entirely). Words seen fewer than times are dropped too. Every surviving word gets an integer id, which is just its frequency rank: id 0 is the most common word. Edit the text and watch the tokens update.

Code map

    
Where each piece of word2vec.py shows up on this page.

    

      load_corpus()
Downloads IMDB and keeps the first  training reviews.

      tokenize() · Vocab
Whitespace split + isalpha() filter, min-count vocab, word ↔ id maps ordered by frequency.

      build_cbow_windows()
numpy sliding windows split into contexts [N, 4] and targets [N].

      CBOW
nn.Embedding → mean → nn.Linear. The entire neural network.

      train()
Adam + CrossEntropyLoss over shuffled batches, records per-epoch loss.

      get_embeddings() · most_similar()
Read out E and rank words by cosine similarity.

      save_embeddings() · save_model()
Artifacts consumed by export_viz.py to build this page's data.
